# Supper club outreach

Short messages for approaching restaurants that already run ticketed dinners:
supper clubs, chef's table nights, tasting menus, brunch parties, wine
pairings. Not everyday table reservations — those don't have a ticket price,
so there's nothing for Zivotix to do.

**Who to approach:** a restaurant that has already run at least one ticketed
event and collected money for it by hand. They already feel the pain, so you
aren't selling them the idea, only a better way to do it.

**Who to skip:** anywhere that only does walk-ins and normal bookings.

---

## 1. First message (WhatsApp or Instagram DM)

Keep it this short. The goal is a reply, not a sale.

> Hi [name], I saw your [supper club / tasting night] — looks great.
>
> Quick question: how are you handling tickets for it at the moment?
>
> I run Zivotix, a ticketing platform. If you're collecting transfers manually
> and tracking names in a note, we can take that off you. Guests pay online,
> get a QR ticket instantly, and you scan them at the door. Money lands in your
> account the day after the event.
>
> Happy to set your next one up free so you can see it, no commitment.

**Why it's phrased this way:** it opens with a question rather than a pitch, so
there's something easy to reply to. And "free so you can see it" is a real
offer you can afford right now — you need the reference customer more than you
need the first commission.

---

## 2. If they reply asking how it works

> Takes about 10 minutes to set up.
>
> You get a page for the dinner with your photos, the menu, and the price. Set
> how many seats. Share the link on Instagram or WhatsApp.
>
> Guests pay by card or Apple Pay, and the ticket hits their inbox straight
> away with a QR code. On the night you open the scanner on your phone and scan
> people in, so no list, no confusion at the door, and nobody gets in twice on
> one ticket.
>
> Afterwards you post photos to your page and the guests who came can comment
> on them. That's the part restaurants tend to like most, it keeps the last
> dinner alive while you're selling the next one.
>
> Payout goes out the day after. [Lagos: straight to your bank account.]
> [Bangkok: by transfer, we handle it manually for now.]
>
> Want me to set up your next one so you can see it before deciding?

---

## 3. If they ask what it costs

Be straight about this. Don't dodge it, and don't promise a rate you can't
sustain — anything under about 5% loses you money on an Apple Pay sale once
the processor takes its cut.

> 10% of ticket sales, nothing upfront, nothing monthly. If you sell nothing,
> you pay nothing.
>
> For your first dinner I'll waive it entirely. I'd rather you try it properly
> than take a fee off one event.

---

## 4. If they say "we just use a WhatsApp group / bank transfers"

> That works until it doesn't. The usual problems are people saying they've
> paid when they haven't, no-shows you can't fill because you found out on the
> night, and having to reconcile transfers by hand afterwards.
>
> With a ticket link, the seat isn't held until the money's in, and you can see
> exactly who's coming at any point.
>
> Not saying you need to change. But if you want to try it on one dinner, I'll
> set it up and you can compare.

---

## What to have ready before you send any of this

- **A real example to point at.** A link to an actual event page is worth more
  than any description. Use one of Eden's if you don't have a restaurant one yet.
- **An honest answer on Thailand payouts.** They're a manual wire, not
  automated. Say so. Getting caught out on this later costs more than the deal.
- **A verified badge on your own organizer account**, so the example page you
  send doesn't look unfinished.

## What not to claim

You have one organizer and twelve tickets sold. Don't imply scale you don't
have — anyone can look at the site. Lead with the offer to run their next
dinner free instead. "You'd be one of the first restaurants on it, so you'll
get my full attention" is both true and more persuasive than a fake number.
